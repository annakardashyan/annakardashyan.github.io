About

I’m a sophomore majoring in Political Science with a minor in Symbolic Systems. I grew up in Anchorage, Alaska, and I’m particularly interested in the intersection of law, politics, neuroscience, and artificial intelligence.

I’ve always enjoyed learning about how government works, and I had the opportunity to serve as a U.S. Senate Page while I was in high school. I’m taking this course because I want to better understand how AI can shape and impact our political institutions and legal systems!

Something I’ve worked on

Last quarter I worked in a neuroscience lab studying how children process visual information, including how vision and memory shape the way they interpret and remember what they see.

Project interests

  1. When should defendants have a right to access the algorithms used to determine their risk or sentence, even when companies claim the technology is proprietary?
  2. Can algorithmic predictions ever constitute reasonable suspicion under the 4th Amendment, particularly when they rely on historically biased policing data?
  3. How meaningful is “human oversight” when judges, police, or government officials consistently defer to algorithmic recommendations?
  4. Who should be legally responsible when an automated system causes harm: the government agency, the technology company, or the official who relied on it?
  5. How is AI changing political campaigns, voter outreach, and the spread of political misinformation?
